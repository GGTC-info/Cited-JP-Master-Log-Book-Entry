Entry title: Rubber Gloves Do Not Fully Hide Fingerprints
Summary: Rubber gloves can reduce direct fingerprint transfer, but they do not guarantee fingerprint concealment. Forensic sources note that gloves can leave glove impressions, transfer residues, preserve prints on the glove surface or inside the glove, and sometimes allow recoverable fingermarks depending on glove material and conditions.  

Source Notes

* NIJ Fingerprint Sourcebook: latent print development uses optical, physical, and chemical methods.  
* Forensic Science International / PubMed: used latex gloves can contain fingermarks and DNA from the wearer.  
* Crime Scene Investigator article: latent prints have been developed from latex/rubber gloves using forensic processing methods.  
* Recent forensic glove study: latent fingerprints can be developed on latex, nitrile, and vinyl gloves.

## Entry

Interesting fun fact: rubber gloves do not actually guarantee that fingerprints are hidden.

## Verified Note

Rubber or latex gloves may reduce direct fingerprint transfer, but forensic sources show that gloves can still preserve or transfer useful evidence, including:

- latent fingermarks on used gloves
- glove impressions on touched surfaces
- residues from the wearer
- possible DNA or trace evidence
- recoverable marks depending on glove type and processing method
